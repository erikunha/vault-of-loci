# Elicit

> [!tldr] In 3 lines
> An AI research assistant that searches papers and writes reports with citations.
> The page says it searches more than 138 million papers and 545,000 clinical trials.
> It claims sentence-level citations for each generated claim.

## Study explanation

The page describes four lines of work. Research agent: gathers evidence from scientific databases, journals, public sources and uploaded documents, and makes cited reports, tables, figures and slides. Search: semantic search over more than 138 million papers and 545,000 clinical trials, without the exact keywords. Research reports: summaries inspired by systematic reviews, which you can customize. Systematic literature review: automates screening and data extraction and partly supports search and reporting. The page claims it finds up to 1,000 relevant papers and analyzes up to 20,000 data points at once, and that it backs generated claims with sentence-level citations.

It helps to find and screen papers by research question. By an image caption on the page, results show relevance and citation count. Citation transparency lets you open the source of each claim, which helps to validate. This is a vendor page: the claims about accuracy and time saved (up to 80%, reported by researchers) are the company's own. Peer review as a filter and a focus on AI papers: not verified. Always open the source and find the arXiv ID before you import a paper.

The page has a Try now button. Whether it needs sign up: not verified. A blog post dated 15 July 2026 mentions an API and an MCP server (title: "The Elicit API and MCP: Powering autonomous research engines"). A blog post linked on the page says Elicit moves from limits per workflow to a monthly usage pool. Values and prices: not verified.

## Key takeaways

- **Remember:** Elicit gives leads with citations. Open each citation and find the arXiv ID before you import.
- **Common confusion:** Sentence-level citations are a claim of the vendor. They make checking easier, they do not replace it.
- **Exercise:** Ask Elicit one research question. Open the source of two claims and check that each says what the report says.
