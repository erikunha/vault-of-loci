# ResearchRabbit

> [!tldr] In 3 lines
> A tool that turns a seed paper into a map of connected ideas.
> It connects to your reference library and learns from what you explore.
> The page does not state price or account needs in the parts read.

## Study explanation

The home page says you use one paper as a seed to see how papers connect, to find what matters and to develop ideas. It talks about bringing your reference library into the tool and exploring how the literature review connects, to find research you missed. It also describes a recommendation engine: as you add related work, new authors and emerging topics, the tool learns and recommends more relevant papers.

It helps to find gaps and neighbours of a key paper: start from a paper you already validated and see what links to it. New authors and emerging topics enter the recommendations, which helps with recency. Citation counts and peer review status: not verified.

Account or key: not verified, the extracted page does not say. Which reference manager is accepted: not verified. Prices and limits are not stated.

## Key takeaways

- **Remember:** Start from a paper you validated and let ResearchRabbit show what connects to it.
- **Common confusion:** Recommendations that learn from your library are not a quality filter. They do not show citations or peer review.
- **Exercise:** Pick one seed paper. Write two neighbours it shows and why each is related.
